#technology/ai/safety

I think the idea of "solving" alignment and then proceeding to live in a transhumanist utopia is leading safety researchers down the wrong path. Empirically, alignment doesn't seem like something that you can "solve". As [Robert Miles](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pYXy-A4siMw) says, if you encode your top 20 values/goals, your 21st value will probably be drastically gone. But this is something with humans as well. Every decision involves tradeoffs, so I think solving "alignment" as many safety researchers think of it, is impossible. Even if you could align models, whose values do you want to align them with? There will have to be some other way to keep models safe. Maybe it builds a world model akin to [this](https://evanjconrad.com/posts/world-models) , and we figure out a way to bias it towards liking humans. On that note, I don't why the model should necessarily be indifferent to humans. Why aren't most humans indifferent to humans?

Also, on the transhumanist utopia, I strongly believe that [all advances in technology just lead to more risk](https://michaelnotebook.com/xrisk/), and there's no reason to believe that the power of AI will be used perfectly for good even if it works. That argument follows a similar one to the technology argument that is argued by Will McAskill in [[What We Owe The Future]].
